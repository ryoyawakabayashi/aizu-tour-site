Must-Visit

Tsuruga Castle

Tsuruga Castle (Aizu-Wakamatsu Castle)

An impregnable castle that endured a fierce siege during the Boshin War (1868-1869, Japan's largest civil war). Features rare red roof tiles on the castle tower, which now serves as a museum. Enjoy panoramic city views from the top floor. A famous cherry blossom spot in spring.

  • Take photos of the castle or with the castle in the background
  • Post to SNS (include your impressions of the visit)

Rinkaku (matcha experience) is available in the same area.

Sightseeing Spots

Sightseeing Spots List

Iimoriyama (Byakkotai Graves)

Historic sites commemorating the Boshin War tragedy, including graves of the 19 Byakkotai warriors and the site of their death. See the Important Cultural Property Sazaedo and the Tonokuchi canal tunnel the warriors used. Panoramic views of Aizu-Wakamatsu from the summit.

Sazaedo (National Important Cultural Property)

A rare wooden hexagonal three-story hall with a double-helix structure. The ascending and descending paths are separate, allowing pilgrims to complete their visit without crossing paths. Official name: Entsu Sansodo.

Oyakuen Garden (Aizu Matsudaira Garden)

Named after the approximately 400 medicinal herbs grown here. A beautiful strolling pond garden with borrowed scenery centered on a heart-shaped pond. Beloved as a villa by successive feudal lords. Enjoy matcha at the Ochaya-goten.

Aizu Samurai Residence

A reconstructed residence of an Aizu clan elder. A history theme park where you can experience the lifestyle, culture, and history of samurai.

Address: 1 Ishiyama, Higashiyama-machi, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Nisshinkan (Aizu Domain School)

Reconstructed highest educational institution of the Aizu domain, where Byakkotai warriors studied. Learn about the education system and history. Notable facilities include one of Japan's largest observatories and a swimming pool called Suibachi.

Showa Natsukashikan (Showa Nostalgia Museum)

Recreates a Japanese street corner from the Showa 30s era (1955-1964), when life was modest but rich in spirit. Touch authentic furniture and items from that time, experiencing a time slip to old Japan.

Address: 1-1-46 Omachi, Aizu-Wakamatsu (along Nanukamachi Street)

Kogai-kuni Shrine

A shrine dedicated to the deity of sericulture protection. Features a sacred tree "Minehari-zakura" over 1,000 years old, with beautiful autumn foliage. Also worshipped for industrial prosperity and traffic safety.

Address: 2-1 Kogai-machi, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Yamada Cotton Weaving

A long-established maker and seller of "Aizu Cotton," a traditional craft. Free tours of the factory where antique looms still operate, discovering the charm of durable cotton products.

Address: 11-5 Nanukamachi, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Aizu-mura (Prayer Village)

Features a vast 60,000-tsubo Japanese garden and a 57m tall "Aizu Jibo Dai-Kannon" statue as its symbol. Climb inside the statue for panoramic views of Aizu.

Address: 7 Dogairi, Asayama, Kawahigashi-machi, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Kagetsutei Garden Museum

A museum utilizing a strolling Japanese garden and storehouse from a sake brewery dating to the Edo period. Enjoy cafe service while viewing the garden in a nationally registered tangible cultural property guest room.

Address: 5-7 Zaimoku-machi 2-chome, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Taketou

Aizu's oldest commercial building, founded in 1624 (Kan'ei 1). A shop selling bamboo crafts and sundries where you can experience making "Aizu Tojin-dako" kites in the charming 160-year-old interior.

Address: 1-2-7 Chuo, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Silk Road Museum

A small museum near Tsuruga Castle housed in a brick warehouse. Displays art collected from the Silk Road and Nanban culture. Features a tearoom and a shop of the traditional Japanese confectionery maker "Aizu Aoi."

Address: 4-6 Ottemachi, Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima

Experience Spots

Experience Spots List

Akabeko Land

An area dedicated to "Akabeko," a famous Aizu craft. Learn about the origins and history of the red cow while enjoying souvenirs and hands-on experiences.

Handcraft Plaza Bansho

A workshop where you can paint papier-mâché akabeko or okiagari-koboshi tumbler dolls. Watch craftsmen at work (near Iimoriyama).

Suehiro Sake Brewery (Ka'ei Kura)

A long-established sake brewery founded in 1850. Tour the historic Ka'ei warehouse and see traditional brewing processes and tools. Tasting, direct sales, and a cafe are popular.

Hoshiban Candle Shop

A painted candle shop with over 300 years of history. Traditional crafts featuring seasonal flowers are displayed and sold. Painting experience available in-store (reservation required).

Restaurants

Restaurants List

Mitsutaya

A local cuisine restaurant renovated from a late Edo period miso warehouse. Famous for its traditional irori (hearth) style where dengaku is grilled with house-made miso.

Shirokujaku Shokudo

A popular restaurant for Aizu's famous sauce katsudon. Extra-large thin pork loin generously covered in sweet-savory sauce. Excellent balance of volume and taste.

Shibukawa Tonya

An inn and restaurant utilizing a Meiji-era seafood wholesaler building. Serves Aizu local cuisine and seasonal ingredients. The retro building is also a highlight.
